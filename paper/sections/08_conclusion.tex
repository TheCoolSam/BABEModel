Dynamic-BABE links opinion dynamics, dyadic trust, exit, and a stylized Suppression Filter. Under our defaults, the filter sharply improves retention (and retention-scaled revenue) while raising \emph{active-agent} polarization by keeping extreme agents who would otherwise exit; full-cohort $\sigma_{\mathrm{all}}$ does not show that rise. Local same-pole triangle purity can fall even while active $\sigma$ stays high---another KPI divergence under suppression. Trust co-evolution separately produces trust segregation. Seed-paired Filter $\times$ Trust tests show \emph{no evidence} that trust attenuates the active-$\sigma$ paradox or materially changes retention gains under these defaults; non-significance is not equivalence.

The practical implication is modest: platform KPIs need not move together. Retention, active-user dispersion, full-cohort dispersion, and same-pole triangle purity can diverge when disagreement is suppressed. Designs that only minimise visible conflict may miss relational outcomes that matter for long-run tolerance. Under our defaults we find \emph{no evidence} that enabling trust co-evolution resolves the active-set trade-off; whether other contact or repair regimes could do so remains an open modelling question beyond the present $2\times2$.
