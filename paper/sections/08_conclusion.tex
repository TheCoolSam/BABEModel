% Conclusion Section

This study has investigated the complex, co-evolutionary feedback loops that govern opinion dynamics and relational structures on social media platforms, exploring the unexpected systemic consequences of deploying content moderation systems under fragile interpersonal trust. By formalizing the Dynamic-BABE (Biased Assimilation \& Behavioral Entrenchment) model, we have demonstrated the emergence of the \textbf{Polarization Paradox}: a critical structural phenomenon where platforms deploy bridging algorithms to successfully suppress conflict and preserve user retention and revenue, but paradoxically lock the social system into an intense, long-term ideological deadlock. By closing the natural exit-valve of ideological extremists without facilitating a path for constructive voice, algorithmic interventions merely treat the symptoms of online polarization while entrenching its structural causes.

Furthermore, our integration of a co-evolving \textbf{Dyadic Trust} framework has highlighted the double-edged nature of interpersonal trust. While trust serves as a valuable constructive buffer within ideological groups, allowing friends to argue and build consensus, its inherent fragility under cross-camp conflict accelerates relational segregation. Relieved of toxic conflict by bridging algorithms, agents are left in mutual isolation, where passive relationship decay slowly and relentlessly erodes remaining cross-cutting ties, resulting in an exceptionally segregated relational landscape with a Trust Segregation Ratio exceeding $37.0$.

Ultimately, our findings call for a fundamental paradigm shift in social media design. If modern platforms are to move beyond the Polarization Paradox, they must abandon the single-minded optimization of short-term engagement and brand safety. Instead, platform architects must implement multi-objective designs that prioritize the preservation and accumulation of relational capital, creating spaces where cross-camp trust can be built on shared saliences, converting toxic outgroup hostility into constructive democratic dialogue.
