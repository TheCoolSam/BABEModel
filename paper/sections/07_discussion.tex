We interpret the Polarization Paradox with Exit--Voice--Loyalty, discuss trust, state conditional implications, summarise robustness, and list limitations.

\subsection{The Polarization Paradox: Exit, Voice, and Loyalty}
\citet{Hirschman1970} distinguishes \textbf{Exit} from \textbf{Voice}. In the Baseline, rejection-zone contact raises frustration and many agents exit---costly for the platform, but also removing some extreme agents from the active set.

The Suppression Filter intercepts many Zone~3 events and reduces exit. Retention and retention-scaled revenue improve; because $\sigma(0)$ already exceeds the stylized cliff, that revenue gain is demographic. The filter often replaces rejection with Zone~2 silence rather than constructive engagement, so it does not build Voice. Closing exit without opening repair leaves extreme agents active, sustaining high \emph{active-agent} polarization while churn falls. Full-cohort $\sigma_{\mathrm{all}}$ does not rise, so the paradox is retention versus survivor dispersion---not population warming.

\subsection{Dyadic Trust}
Trust is double-edged. Agreement raises ingroup trust (mean $0.9808$ in Trust Only); conflict and asymmetry ($\delta_-/\delta_+ = 2.5$) following~\citet{Slovic1993} collapse outgroup trust (mean $0.0432$). With passive decay, trust segregates (mean ratio $31.28$; median $26.76$). These patterns come from enabling trust co-evolution, not from the Filter contrast. Trust shifts $w_{ij}$ before SJT zone classification (source-credibility weighting). Assimilation and repulsion step sizes are clamped to $[0,1]$ so high trust cannot produce DeGroot overshoot.

\subsection{Conditional Implications}
Curation systems often optimise engagement and retention. Our results suggest those objectives need not track cohesion among remaining users. If designers also care about cross-camp relational capital, suppression alone is an incomplete instrument. That statement is a conditional implication from a theoretical laboratory, not a prescription for any named platform.

\subsection{Robustness}
OFAT sweeps over $\beta_{\mathrm{mean}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{assim}}$, $T_c$, $K$, $\gamma_T$, $E$, and $m_e$ (five replications per cell; $N=500$) preserve the qualitative filter--churn contrast at interior points; exceptions are identifiable boundaries (very low $\beta$; $E=0$; near-perfect $E=1$). See \texttt{sensitivity.py} and \texttt{output/sensitivity\_results.csv}.

Additional checks (Results) are lower-power supplements: (i) placebo heal without intercept is weaker than intercept+heal; (ii) drop without heal matches no-heal intercept and is distinct from M0; (iii) Dynamic-BABE differs from HK / biased-DeGroot baselines in linking suppression to exit; (iv) Filter OFF/ON remains directionally similar on Watts--Strogatz under our defaults.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\begin{enumerate*}
    \item \textbf{Symmetric trust:} $T_{ij}=T_{ji}$.
    \item \textbf{Static undirected topology:} BA/WS graphs are fixed; real follower graphs are directed and rewiring.
    \item \textbf{Low-dimensional issues:} $K=2$.
    \item \textbf{Pole edge case:} $\mathrm{sign}(\overline{O}_i)=0$ is a distinct pole (rare under bipolar init).
    \item \textbf{No field fit:} Parameters are not estimated from a named platform dataset.
    \item \textbf{Stylized filter and revenue:} Revenue $\approx$ retention under a pre-polarized cliff; not an audited production system.
\end{enumerate*}
Despite these limits, the model is a transparent testbed for how suppression and fragile trust jointly shape polarization and retention.
