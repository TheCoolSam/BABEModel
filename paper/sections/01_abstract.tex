Ideological polarization on social media can harm both public discourse and platform economics through user exit. Platforms often respond with suppression-oriented feed filters that reduce exposure to confrontational content in Social Judgment Theory's latitude of rejection. We study long-run effects with Dynamic-BABE (Biased Assimilation \& Behavioral Entrenchment), a stylized ABM combining biased assimilation, social-judgment zones, exit, and co-evolving dyadic trust. A $2\times2$ design crosses the Suppression Filter (ON/OFF) with trust co-evolution (ON/OFF) over $30$ replications per cell ($N=500$, $200$ steps). We find a Polarization Paradox on the \emph{active-user} KPI: the filter cuts cumulative churn from $39.18\%$ to $0.38\%$ and raises step-wise revenue from \$121.64 to \$199.24---revenue tracking retention under a pre-polarized cliff that does not switch---yet active-agent polarization rises from $0.9550$ to $0.9904$. Full-cohort $\sigma_{\mathrm{all}}$ does not show that rise ($0.9904$ vs.\ $0.9905$), indicating survivorship. Very large Cohen's $d$ values reflect nearly separated distributions. Enabling trust alone produces trust segregation (mean ratio $31.28$; median $26.76$). Seed-paired Filter $\times$ Trust tests show retention gains remain robust when trust is active. In short, suppressing disagreement can protect retention without improving cohesion among survivors.
