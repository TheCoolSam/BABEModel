Ideological polarization on social media can harm both public discourse and platform economics, notably through user exit and lower advertising value. Platforms often respond with suppression-oriented feed filters that reduce exposure to confrontational, cross-cutting content in what Social Judgment Theory calls the latitude of rejection. We study long-run effects of such a filter with Dynamic-BABE (Biased Assimilation \& Behavioral Entrenchment), a \emph{stylized} agent-based model that combines biased assimilation, social-judgment zones, and co-evolving dyadic trust. A $2\times2$ design crosses the filter (ON/OFF; factorial labels Bridge ON/OFF for continuity) with trust co-evolution (ON/OFF) over $30$ replications per cell ($N=500$, $200$ steps). We find a Polarization Paradox on the active-user KPI: the filter cuts cumulative churn from $39.18\%$ to $0.38\%$ ($d = 23.97$, $p < 0.001$) and raises step-wise revenue from \$121.64 to \$199.24 ($d = -23.97$, $p < 0.001$)---with revenue tracking retention under a pre-polarized brand-safety cliff that does not switch in the main design---yet final polarization among active agents rises from $0.9550$ to $0.9904$ ($d = -1.28$, $p < 0.001$). Full-cohort polarization $\sigma_{\mathrm{all}}$ (churned agents retain last opinions) does not show that rise ($0.9904$ vs.\ $0.9905$), indicating survivorship in the active set. Very large $d$ values reflect nearly separated distributions across conditions. Separately, enabling trust alone (Trust Only vs.\ Baseline) produces strong trust segregation ($35.61 \pm 31.50$; median $= 28.58$; $d = -1.55$, $p < 0.001$). Seed-paired Filter $\times$ Trust tests show that retention gains remain robust when trust is active, while relational moderation is modest. In short, interventions that reduce exit need not improve cohesion when disagreement is suppressed and trust is fragile.
